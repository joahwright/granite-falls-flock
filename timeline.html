Granite Falls record

How the system got here.

A timeline built from the executed contract, City payment records, permit material, internal correspondence and Council records.

Contract to current review

From purchase to scrutiny.

Oct. 17, 2024

Contract executed

Granite Falls and Flock execute the Order Form and Master Services Agreement. The Order Form covers FlockOS and seven Falcon cameras.

Nov. 6, 2024

City pays initial invoice

Flock invoiced Granite Falls for seven cameras plus implementation. City payment records show $26,784.05 paid on Nov. 6.

Nov. 2024

Right-of-way plans

Flock location drawings are dated Nov. 4. Right-of-Way Permit 2024-019 states six solar-powered ALPR installations in City right-of-way.

June 13, 2025

Stanwood records dispute enters Granite Falls correspondence

After Stanwood paused Flock during a large public-records dispute, Granite Falls officials and counsel discussed the requester and whether Granite Falls would face similar requests.

June 25–26, 2025

Counsel asks about the requester

City counsel asks whether Granite Falls had received a Flock-data request from “Jose.” Chief Dalton says it had not and provides four examples of successful Flock use.

June 30, 2025

Officials discuss public-records strategy and legislation

Counsel circulates a template letter seeking a specific Washington Public Records Act exemption for ALPR data. In the follow-up thread, City Manager Brent Kirk says Granite Falls is “sitting back trying to stay under the radar as long as possible” and discusses supporting a legislative change.

July 2025

Granite Falls asks Flock for a records solution

Jeff Balentine contacts Flock Legal about a cloud-based method for handling large Flock public-records requests and reducing the risk of delayed production.

Aug. 27, 2025

Renewal becomes the police budget priority

Chief Tom Dalton’s 2026 budget memorandum lists Flock contract renewal first and calls it the department’s “number one priority.”

Sept. 2025

City maintains a 100th Street camera view

City correspondence says the Police Chief asked Public Works to address a tree blocking a Flock camera on 100th Street.

Sept. 30, 2025

Council opposition is documented

Councilmember Bruce Straughn writes that he opposed the original Flock contract and remained opposed to the system.

Nov.–Dec. 2025

Council discussion and legislative concerns continue

Straughn asks for another Council discussion. Chief Dalton later contacts the Association of Washington Cities to discuss proposed retention and warrant rules.

Mar. 30, 2026

Washington ALPR law changes

Washington’s new ALPR law takes effect, adding a general 21-day retention limit, restricted-location rules, sharing controls, registration, audit trails and annual internal audits.

May 2026

Local public-records investigation begins

A Granite Falls public-records request begins producing the contracts, payment records, deployment material and internal correspondence used throughout this site.

Aug. 2026

Flock returns to Council review

Granite Falls Council agendas place Flock contract review and SB 6002 compliance back before the Council.
