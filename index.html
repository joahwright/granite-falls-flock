Granite Falls · automated license plate readers

What Flock is. Why it matters. What happens next.

Flock Safety Falcon cameras are automated license plate readers. They photograph passing vehicles and create time-and-location records that can be searched for investigative purposes. Granite Falls purchased seven Falcon cameras.

The renewal window is closing

Late September is the decision point.

The executed MSA says either party must give notice of non-renewal at least 30 days before the end of the then-current term. The original annual service period shown on the invoice runs October 23 through October 22.

Apparent deadline: September 22, 2026

If the City does not send timely notice of non-renewal, the agreement can continue into another renewal term without executing a brand-new contract.

This September 22 date is the practical deadline calculated from the MSA's 30-day notice clause and the October 23–October 22 annual service period. It assumes the current renewal term remains on the same anniversary dates.
Why residents should care

The system records ordinary travel at scale.

ALPRs do not need facial recognition to create location information tied to a vehicle. A license plate, timestamp and camera location can connect a registered vehicle with a place and time.

The documented local story

Public attention arrived after deployment.

The public record now shows several threads at once: Granite Falls adopted Flock with limited public attention; officials later discussed public-records exposure and ways to handle it—reportedly out of concern for protecting citizen ALPR data from public-records requests, according to City Manager Jeff Balentine in a September 2026 meeting with this project; Washington later enacted new ALPR restrictions; the camera near the high school raises a specific statutory question; and the contract continues automatically unless the City acts on time.

What this project is doing

Testing the concerns against public institutions.

This project has gone beyond publishing records. Concerns identified through the documents have been raised with relevant public bodies and elected officials.

Washington Attorney General's Office

Raised the Granite Falls High School ALPR-location concern. A report or complaint is not a finding that a violation occurred.

Granite Falls School District

Submitted a Public Records Act request seeking records about notice, consultation, placement, privacy concerns and compliance involving the camera near the high school.

State legislators

Contacted lawmakers regarding Washington's ALPR framework and local implementation.

DeFlock coordination

Shared research and coordinated with DeFlock. DeFlock is the only outside organization this project describes as a partner.
