Granite Falls · automated license plate readers

What Flock is. Why it matters. What happens next.

Flock Safety Falcon cameras are automated license plate readers. They photograph passing vehicles and create time-and-location records that can be searched for investigative purposes. Granite Falls purchased seven Falcon cameras.

Renewal timeline corrected

The working non-renewal window is mid-January 2027.

The executed MSA says that, unless the Order Form states otherwise, the contract term begins with the first installation of Flock hardware. The City’s February 4, 2026 Flock presentation identified February 13 as the renewal date. After that conflicted with the earlier October-based interpretation, City Manager Jeff Balentine confirmed that the MSA Terms and Conditions are correct.

Working renewal date: February 13, 2027

The MSA requires notice of non-renewal at least 30 days before the end of the then-current term. This site therefore treats mid-January 2027 as the operative notice window and uses January 13 as a conservative planning date while recognizing that the contracting parties control the exact day count.

Council can direct the cameras disabled or pursue ending the agreement at any time. The mid-January notice window matters because timely non-renewal avoids the next annual renewal and fee; action after that point would require another contractual path or a negotiated exit.
Why residents should care

The system records ordinary travel at scale.

ALPRs do not need facial recognition to create location information tied to a vehicle. A license plate, timestamp and camera location can connect a registered vehicle with a place and time.

The documented local story

Public attention arrived after deployment.

The public record now shows several threads at once: Granite Falls adopted Flock with limited public attention; officials later discussed public-records exposure and ways to handle it—reportedly out of concern for protecting citizen ALPR data from public-records requests, according to City Manager Jeff Balentine in a September 2026 meeting with this project; Washington later enacted new ALPR restrictions; the camera near the high school was re-aimed but not physically moved after the new law took effect; and the City has completed its own records production while key operational records remain with the Snohomish County Sheriff’s Office.

September 2026 update

The City file is complete. The SCSO operational record is not.

City Clerk Darla Wilkins described Installment 6 as the City’s sixth and final production and said the City had provided all responsive records currently in its possession. The installment adds Council packets, police reports and ALPR compliance presentations, but it does not contain the requested SCSO query-by-query audit, search and access logs.

The City directed several remaining operational categories to the Snohomish County Sheriff’s Office, including Attorney General registration material, audit/search/access logs, records identifying outside agencies that queried or accessed Granite Falls ALPR data, and complaints or inquiries held by SCSO. A separate SCSO public-records request also seeks policies, access information, correspondence and camera-status records. SCSO’s current estimate for the next installment is November 23, 2026.

What this project is doing

Testing the concerns against public institutions.

This project has gone beyond publishing records. Concerns identified through the documents have been raised with relevant public bodies and elected officials.

Washington Attorney General's Office

Raised the Granite Falls High School ALPR-location concern. A report or complaint is not a finding that a violation occurred.

Granite Falls School District

Submitted a Public Records Act request seeking records about notice, consultation, placement, privacy concerns and compliance involving the camera near the high school.

State legislators

Contacted lawmakers regarding Washington's ALPR framework and local implementation.

DeFlock coordination

Shared research and coordinated with DeFlock. DeFlock is the only outside organization this project describes as a partner.
