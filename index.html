Public records · Granite Falls, Washington

Get the Flock out.

Granite Falls contracted for seven Flock Safety automated license plate reader cameras. This project tracks the contract, the money, the camera locations, the City's own correspondence, and the rules now governing the system under Washington law.

01 / Campaign

What has happened so far.

The campaign's goal is to end Granite Falls' use of Flock Safety ALPR cameras through public oversight and a documented Council decision.

Public records

City records obtained

Contracts, invoices, payment records, deployment plans, internal correspondence and Flock-related Council material have been reviewed and organized here.

Public comment

Concerns placed on the record

Flock has been raised directly with the City through public comment and Council meetings.

Meeting attendance

Contract review followed

City agendas and packets are being tracked as Flock, renewal and Washington-law compliance return to Council.

Public outreach

Records made easier to read

The website, flyers and community outreach are intended to put the City's own records in front of residents without requiring them to sort through large production files.

02 / Local record

What the documents show.

PurchaseThe October 2024 invoice lists seven Falcon cameras and implementation services. City payment records show $26,784.05 paid to Flock Group.
LocationsThe right-of-way permit states six solar-powered ALPR installations and includes a location map and plan sheets for those sites.
Public recordsInternal emails show City officials, police and counsel discussing the records problems other Washington jurisdictions were encountering with Flock data.
RenewalChief Tom Dalton's August 2025 budget memo identified Flock contract renewal as the police department's first listed funding priority.
2026 lawESSB 6002 added a general 21-day retention limit, restricted-location rules, sharing controls and audit requirements for ALPR systems.
